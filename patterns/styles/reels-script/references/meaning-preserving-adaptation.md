# Meaning-preserving competitor adaptation

Use this reference only when the user supplies or approves a competitor script and asks for an original ONai version that preserves its meaning.

## Preserve the work performed by the source

Before drafting, list the source's distinct semantic units in order. Common units include:

- the concrete benefit or threat promised by the opening;
- the central position;
- named parts of a system and the relationship between them;
- the consequence of not understanding the system;
- the practical action the viewer can take;
- the learning or decision principle;
- the realistic time horizon or expectation reset;
- the single response requested in the CTA.

Every distinct unit is required unless it is inaccurate, unsafe, contradicted by a newer user decision or replaced by a genuinely equivalent explanation. Do not compress a useful system explanation into one clever example if that removes named parts or causal links.

## Rebuild rather than disguise

Keep the explanatory sequence when its order is what makes the lesson understandable. Rewrite the language, sentence rhythm and examples; add one ONai-specific test, decision rule or demonstration. Do not copy a competitor's distinctive sentences, character, graphic assets or branded CTA. Do not perform shallow synonym substitution.

For the approved Pronin-inspired explanatory mode, the useful grammar is:

1. tangible benefit or recognizable risk;
2. firm central claim;
3. a visible map of the system in plain language;
4. what goes wrong when the viewer lacks that map;
5. one way to inspect, test or ask a better question;
6. a realistic conclusion about progress;
7. one continuation, discussion or owned-channel CTA.

This is a functional grammar, not a sentence template. Omit steps that are absent from the supplied source; do not add artificial drama merely to complete the pattern.

## Pedagogical density test

- Each paragraph introduces a new object, relationship, consequence or action.
- Technical nouns receive short functional explanations when the audience needs them: what the interface shows, what the server processes, what the API connects, what authorization permits and what the database stores.
- The viewer can retell the system after one listen.
- The practical instruction contains a verb and an observable check.
- The ending follows from the lesson rather than switching to a generic subscription request.

## Final semantic diff

Compare the source spine and draft side by side in working notes. Mark every source unit as preserved, truth-corrected, replaced by an equivalent, or removed by explicit user direction. If a unit is simply absent, revise the script. Do not expose this checklist in the recording text.
